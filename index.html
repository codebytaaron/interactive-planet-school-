Written description

Planet Overview & Geological Features

▾

Introducing Veyra

Veyra orbits Ostra, an orange dwarf star with about 75% of the Sun's mass. At 0.62 AU it receives roughly 78% of the sunlight Earth gets. That places it inside Ostra's habitable zone, where liquid water can stay on the surface. It is 1.12 times Earth's mass, so the gravity is slightly stronger (1.05 g), and one day lasts 26 hours. One global ocean, the Vael Ocean, covers 74% of the surface. Two main continents rise out of it: Auren in the west and Mervath in the east. There is also an ice-covered polar landmass and several volcanic island chains. The climate is mild, averaging 16 °C, with plum-colored forests, red volcanic soils, golden deserts and wide coastal wetlands.

The engine: active plate tectonics

Veyra is bigger than Earth, so its interior holds on to more heat: leftover heat from when the planet formed, plus heat from radioactive uranium, thorium and potassium decaying. That heat drives convection currents in the mantle. Hot, soft rock slowly rises, cools, spreads out and sinks again. The rigid lithosphere on top is broken into seven plates (Auren, Kesh, Sundra, Mervath, Vael, Cinder and Boreal), and the convection drags them along at 2–9 cm per year. Below the mantle, a liquid iron-nickel outer core churns and creates a strong magnetic field. Turn on Plates and Plate motion in the layer bar to see every boundary and which way it moves.

Divergent boundaries (plates pulling apart)

The Seam Ridge is a mid-ocean ridge where the Sundra and Auren plates spread about 6 cm per year. Magma rises into the gap, cools into new basalt seafloor and builds an underwater mountain chain. Seawater soaks into the hot rock and comes back out at the Seam Vent Gardens loaded with dissolved minerals. On land, the Hollow Rift is slowly tearing Mervath apart. The ground has dropped into a long rift valley holding deep lakes, and in a few million years seawater will flood it and a new ocean will be born.

Convergent boundaries (plates colliding)

Ocean–continent: The Sundra plate is made of dense, cold basalt, so it subducts (dives) under the lighter Mervath continent at the Ember Trench, which is 9.4 km deep. As the slab sinks, it releases water, which lowers the melting point of the mantle above it. The magma that forms rises to build the Ember Arc of volcanoes, including Mount Kaelor (6.1 km). Ocean–ocean: Where the Vael plate subducts under the Cinder plate, a curved chain of volcanic islands has formed, the Cinder Isles. Continent–continent: About 40 million years ago the Kesh block crashed into Auren. Continental crust is too buoyant to subduct, so it crumpled, folded and stacked upward into the Crown Range. It reaches 8.4 km and is still rising a few millimeters every year, with frequent earthquakes.

Transform boundary (plates sliding past)

The Shearline Fault is where two plates grind sideways past each other. No crust is created or destroyed. Instead, stress builds up and is released as shallow earthquakes, and the fault offsets the Seam Ridge so the two ridge pieces don't line up.

Hotspot

The Lantern Isles sit above a mantle plume that stays in one place while the Vael plate slides over it. The newest island is an active volcano. Each older island farther northwest has been worn down more, and the oldest ones have sunk below the waves as seamounts. The chain works like a record of which way the plate has moved.

How tectonics keeps Veyra alive

  • Climate regulation: subduction carries carbon-rich seafloor sediments down into the mantle, and volcanoes later release that carbon as CO₂. This long-term carbon–silicate cycle works like a thermostat that keeps Veyra from freezing over or overheating (see Section 2).
  • Material recycling: the rock cycle never stops. New crust forms at ridges and old crust is destroyed at trenches, so fresh, mineral-rich rock is always being lifted up where it can be weathered into nutrients.
  • Habitats: mountains, rifts, island arcs and hotspots create very different environments, from rift lakes and coral-like reefs to rich volcanic soils and deep-sea vents.
  • Protection: the churning interior that drives the plates also powers the magnetic field, which shields the atmosphere and living things from Ostra's stellar wind.

Landforms present

Fold mountains (Crown Range), stratovolcanoes (Mount Kaelor), volcanic island arcs (Cinder Isles), shield-volcano hotspot islands (Lantern Isles), a deep-sea trench (Ember Trench), a mid-ocean ridge (Seam Ridge), a rift valley with lakes (Hollow Rift), a broad ocean basin and abyssal plain (Great Vael Basin), desert dunes (Ashen Dunes), a canyon (Glasswind Canyon), a river delta (Silvermouth Delta), sea cliffs and sea stacks (Teeth of Ryn), glacial valleys (Frostmere Valleys) and polar ice caps.

Atmosphere, Water & Weathering

▾

The atmosphere

Veyra's air is 75% nitrogen, 22% oxygen and 1.4% argon, with water vapor (about 0.9%) and carbon dioxide (0.06%). That is about 600 ppm of CO₂, a little more than Earth. Because Ostra is dimmer than our Sun, the thicker 1.3-bar atmosphere and extra greenhouse gases trap just enough heat to keep the average temperature at 16 °C. Oxygen high in the atmosphere forms an ozone layer that blocks harmful UV light. The magnetic field deflects the stellar wind so it can't strip the atmosphere away, which is what happened to Mars.

Energy sources

Veyra's main energy source is its star, Ostra. Its orange light powers photosynthesis, heats the ocean so water evaporates, and warms the equator more than the poles. That uneven heating creates winds and ocean currents. There are also two backup sources of energy. Geothermal heat from the interior powers deep-sea vents, where entire ecosystems live without sunlight. Tidal energy from the two moons, Ryn and Pell, stirs the oceans and lifts nutrients up from the deep water.

Where the water is

About 96% of Veyra's water is in the Vael Ocean, which averages 3.9 km deep. Roughly 2% is frozen in the Boreal Ice Cap and the Austral sea ice. The rest is groundwater in aquifers under Auren, lakes like the Hollow Rift lakes, rivers like the Silvermouth, and water vapor and clouds in the air.

The water cycle turn on “Processes”

(1) Evaporation: Ostra heats the Vael Ocean, and water evaporates. The salt stays behind, so this step naturally purifies the water into fresh water. (2) Transport: steady westerly winds push the moist air east toward Auren. (3) Condensation: when the air hits the Crown Range it is forced upward. It expands, cools and condenses into thick clouds (this is called orographic lift). (4) Precipitation: more than 3,000 mm of rain falls each year on the windward Verdant Slopes. (5) Runoff, infiltration and transpiration: the water collects into the Silvermouth River, soaks into the ground as groundwater, or is pulled up by plants and released from their leaves, and eventually all of it flows back to the sea. On the far side of the mountains, the air sinks, warms up and dries out. That makes a rain shadow, which is why the Ashen Dunes desert sits right next to a rainforest. It's a clear example of the geosphere changing the atmosphere.

Why water's properties make life possible

  • Universal solvent: water molecules are polar, so they dissolve salts, minerals and nutrients. That lets rivers carry phosphorus to the sea and lets cells move chemicals around.
  • High specific heat: the ocean absorbs huge amounts of heat during the day and in summer and releases it slowly. This keeps coastal temperatures steady and spreads heat toward the poles through currents.
  • Ice floats: ice is less dense than liquid water, so it forms a floating, insulating lid on polar seas and rift lakes. Life keeps swimming underneath even in winter.
  • Cohesion & adhesion: water molecules stick to each other and to surfaces, so water can climb up through plant tissues by capillary action, all the way to the top of the 90-meter plum trees.
  • Wide liquid range: at 1.3 bar, water doesn't boil until about 107 °C. That keeps it liquid over a huge range of temperatures, so chemical reactions always have a medium to happen in.

Weathering: breaking rock down

Mechanical weathering: In the Crown Range and Frostmere Valleys, water seeps into cracks and freezes. Ice expands by 9%, which pries the rock apart (frost wedging). Roots of plum forests grow into cracks and widen them (root wedging). In the Ashen Dunes, hot days and cold nights make rock expand and contract until it flakes apart, while blowing sand sandblasts it. Chemical weathering: CO₂ dissolves in rain to make weak carbonic acid, which slowly dissolves silicate and limestone rock. Oxygen oxidizes (rusts) iron in the volcanic basalt, which is why the Kaelor Ashlands are brick red. Hydrolysis turns feldspar into clay, which makes soil better at holding water.

Erosion: moving it somewhere else

Water: the Silvermouth River has cut a steep V-shaped valley through the mountains and carries tons of sediment to the fan-shaped Silvermouth Delta. Wind and flash floods: these carved the 1.2 km-deep Glasswind Canyon. Glaciers: moving ice scraped out the U-shaped Frostmere Valleys. Waves: because Veyra has two moons, its tidal range can be twice Earth's. The pounding surf has carved tall cliffs, arches and sea stacks called the Teeth of Ryn. Gravity: landslides on the steep Crown Range slopes dump loose rock into the rivers.

Nutrient cycling & climate control

Weathering frees phosphorus, potassium, calcium and iron that were locked inside rock. Erosion delivers them by river to the delta and the coast, where they feed blooms of plankton and wetland plants. When those organisms die, decomposers return the nutrients to the soil and seafloor. Some sediment gets buried, carried down into a subduction zone, melted, and brought back up by volcanoes as fresh rock that gets weathered again. That loop never stops. Weathering also acts as Veyra's thermostat. When the planet warms, more rain falls and weathering speeds up, pulling CO₂ out of the air into rock, which cools the planet. When it cools, weathering slows down and volcanic CO₂ builds up again, which warms it back up.

Life Ingredients

▾

Veyra includes all 8 of the 8 essential ingredients for life, more than the required 6. Each one below explains how that ingredient is built into the planet's systems, not just listed.

💧Liquid water

Vael Ocean · rivers · rift lakes · aquifers · clouds

Water is where every Veyran cell does its chemistry. It dissolves nutrients and moves them around inside organisms and across the planet. Veyra's distance from Ostra, its thick greenhouse atmosphere and its heat-storing ocean all work together to keep most of the water liquid. Ostra-powered evaporation is constantly recycling it, the magnetic field stops the stellar wind from stripping it away, and volcanoes keep releasing new water vapor from the mantle.

☀️Energy

Ostra's light · geothermal vents · tidal mixing

Life needs energy to build and run its cells. On land and in the sunlit upper ocean, plum-pigmented plants and plankton capture Ostra's orange light through photosynthesis. As a K-dwarf, Ostra will shine steadily for about 30 billion years, far longer than our Sun. In the dark deep sea, microbes at the Seam Vent Gardens use chemosynthesis, getting energy from hydrogen sulfide coming out of the vents. That supply keeps going as long as the interior stays hot and the plates keep moving.

⚫Carbon

air (CO₂) · ocean (dissolved) · limestone · living things

Carbon forms the backbone of every organic molecule: sugars, proteins and DNA. Plants pull CO₂ from the air to build their tissues. Animals eat them and breathe CO₂ back out. Ocean organisms lock carbon into shells, which pile up into limestone. Over millions of years, that limestone is subducted at the Ember Trench and returned to the air by Mount Kaelor. This is the carbon cycle that keeps both life and the climate balanced.

🌫️Nitrogen

75% of the air · soils · ocean

Organisms need nitrogen to build proteins and DNA, but the N₂ gas in the air is very hard for them to use. On Veyra, lightning in the stormy Crown Range clouds and nitrogen-fixing microbes living on plum-tree roots convert it into nitrates that dissolve in soil water. Decomposers return nitrogen to the soil when organisms die, and other bacteria release it back into the air. Volcanoes also add fresh nitrogen from the mantle.

🫧Oxygen

22% of the air · dissolved in seawater · in water & rocks

Oxygen lets animals run cellular respiration, the most efficient way to release energy from food. Up high, it forms the ozone shield that blocks harmful UV light. Photosynthesis by forests and ocean plankton constantly replaces the oxygen that animals use up. Cold, wave-churned polar water and the tidal mixing from the two moons dissolve oxygen into the deep ocean so sea life can breathe there too.

⚛️Hydrogen

water (H₂O) · organic molecules · vent fluids

Hydrogen is part of water and of nearly every biological molecule. Hydrogen bonds are what hold DNA's two strands together and give water its special properties. Most of Veyra's hydrogen is locked safely inside water. Veyra's stronger gravity, cold upper atmosphere and magnetic field stop it from leaking into space. Vent fluids at the Seam Ridge also release hydrogen gas that deep-sea microbes use for food.

🪨Phosphorus

volcanic & mountain rock (apatite) · soils · delta sediment

Phosphorus is part of DNA and ATP, the molecule cells use to carry energy. It doesn't exist as a gas, so it can only enter the biosphere when rock is weathered. Veyra's young, actively rising mountains and fresh volcanic rock make sure that supply never runs out. Rain dissolves phosphorus out of rock, the Silvermouth carries it to the delta, and tectonics eventually lifts the buried sediment back up to be weathered again.

🟡Sulfur

volcanic gases · hydrothermal vents · seawater sulfate

Sulfur helps proteins fold into the right shapes so they work. Volcanoes like Kaelor release sulfur gases that rain washes into soils and the sea. At the vents, hydrogen sulfide is both a nutrient and an energy source for chemosynthetic microbes. Subduction carries sulfur-rich sediments down so that volcanoes can release them again.

Rubric · Integration of Systems

How Veyra's Systems Work Together

▾

None of Veyra's systems could keep life going alone. Each one feeds the others in loops:

  • Geosphere → Atmosphere: volcanoes on the Ember Arc release CO₂, water vapor and nitrogen, which created the air and keep it replenished. The Crown Range blocks wind and creates a rain shadow.
  • Atmosphere → Hydrosphere: Ostra-heated winds drive evaporation and ocean currents, and CO₂ dissolves in rain to make carbonic acid.
  • Hydrosphere → Geosphere: rain, rivers, ice and waves weather and erode rock. Water carried down by subducting slabs lowers the mantle's melting point and creates magma.
  • Geosphere → Biosphere: weathered rock supplies phosphorus and other minerals. Volcanic ash builds the fertile Ashlands, and vents feed deep-sea life.
  • Biosphere → Atmosphere: plum forests and plankton release O₂ and take in CO₂. Transpiration adds water vapor that turns into more rain.
  • Biosphere → Geosphere: roots and lichens speed up weathering, and shells pile up into limestone.

Follow one carbon atom

Mount Kaelor erupts a CO₂ molecule → a plum tree on the Verdant Slopes absorbs it → the leaf falls and decomposes, and rain carries the carbon as carbonic acid into the Silvermouth → it reaches the sea, where a shell-building plankton uses it → the shell sinks and becomes limestone on the Sundra plate → the plate carries it to the Ember Trench and subducts it → it melts and erupts from Kaelor again. All four spheres, one loop.

What if the plates stopped? Volcanoes would stop releasing CO₂ while weathering kept pulling it out of the air. The mountains would erode flat, nutrients would run out, and Veyra would cool into a frozen planet. Tectonics is what holds the whole system together.

Inside a Subduction Zone

▾

Dense oceanic crust sinks under lighter continental crust. Water carried down with the slab lowers the melting point of the mantle above it, so magma forms and rises to feed Kaelor's eruptions. Those eruptions build new land, release CO₂ and deposit fertile ash. This is how a geosphere process directly supports the atmosphere and biosphere.
