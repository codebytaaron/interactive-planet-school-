Designing a Life-Sustaining Planet

Meet Veyra

Veyra is our made-up planet that can support life. It orbits an orange star called Ostra. It has oceans, two continents, active plate tectonics and an atmosphere that keeps it warm and protected. Our goal was to design a planet where the geosphere, hydrosphere, atmosphere and biosphere all work together.

How Veyra is different from Earth

  • An orange star. Ostra is smaller and dimmer than the Sun, so Veyra orbits closer to it (0.62 AU) to stay warm enough for liquid water.
  • Thicker air. Air pressure is 1.3 times Earth's, and the extra greenhouse effect makes up for the dimmer star.
  • Two moons. Ryn and Pell create bigger, more complicated tides, which means stronger waves and more ocean mixing.
  • Dark red-purple plants. Ostra gives off mostly red and orange light, so Veyra's plants evolved darker pigments to absorb more of it. From space, the forests look dark reddish-brown instead of green.
Section 1

Planet Overview and Geological Features

What is Veyra like?

Veyra is a rocky planet a little bigger than Earth, with gravity 1.05 times as strong. It orbits Ostra in the star's habitable zone, where the temperature allows liquid water. Most of the surface is one big ocean, the Vael Ocean. There are two continents, Auren and Mervath, plus an icy land mass at the north pole and a few island chains. The climate is mild, about 16 °C on average, with rainforests, grasslands, deserts, wetlands and ice caps.

Does Veyra have active plate tectonics?

Yes. Because Veyra is bigger than Earth, its interior has stayed hot. Heat from its formation and from radioactive elements decaying causes convection currents in the mantle: hot rock rises, cools and sinks again in a slow loop. Those currents move the crust, which is broken into seven plates (Auren, Kesh, Sundra, Mervath, Vael, Cinder and Boreal). The plates move 2 to 9 cm per year. Choose the Plate boundaries layer to see all of them.

How do the plates interact?

Divergent · mid-ocean ridge

Seam Ridge: two ocean plates pull apart. Magma rises into the gap and cools into new seafloor, building an underwater mountain range. Hot water vents form along it.

Divergent · continental rift

Hollow Rift: Mervath is slowly splitting in two. The land in the middle dropped down and formed a long valley with deep lakes. Someday the ocean will flood in.

Convergent · ocean–continent

Ember Trench: the dense ocean plate (Sundra) sinks under the lighter continent (Mervath). This is called subduction. The sinking plate releases water that melts the mantle above it, and the magma rises to form volcanoes like Mount Kaelor.

Convergent · ocean–ocean

Cinder Isles: one ocean plate sinks under another, creating a curved chain of volcanic islands next to a deep trench.

Convergent · continent–continent

Crown Range: two continents crashed into each other. Neither one sinks, so the crust folds and pushes up into Veyra's tallest mountains (8.4 km). They are still rising and cause earthquakes.

Transform

Shearline Fault: two plates slide past each other sideways. No crust is made or destroyed, but the plates get stuck and then slip, causing earthquakes.

Hotspot

Lantern Isles: a plume of hot mantle stays in one place while the plate moves over it, making a line of volcanic islands. The active island is at one end, and the older islands are more eroded.

How does tectonics help life on Veyra?

  • It controls the climate. Subduction carries carbon from the seafloor down into the mantle, and volcanoes release it back into the air as CO₂. This keeps the amount of greenhouse gas balanced over millions of years, so Veyra doesn't freeze or overheat.
  • It recycles materials. Old crust is destroyed at trenches and new crust forms at ridges. Mountains keep pushing up fresh rock that weathers into nutrients like phosphorus.
  • It creates habitats. Mountains, rift lakes, volcanic soil, islands and deep-sea vents all give life different places to live.
  • It protects the planet. The same hot, moving interior keeps Veyra's liquid iron core flowing, which creates a magnetic field that blocks harmful radiation from Ostra.

Section 2

Atmosphere, Water and Weathering

The atmosphere

Veyra's air is about 75% nitrogen, 22% oxygen, 1% argon, plus water vapor and a small amount of carbon dioxide (0.06%). The air pressure is 1.3 times Earth's. The thicker air and slightly higher CO₂ trap more heat, which is needed because Ostra is dimmer than our Sun. An ozone layer blocks harmful UV light. The magnetic field protects the atmosphere from being blown away by stellar wind, which is what happened to Mars.

Energy source

Veyra's main energy source is its star, Ostra. Its light powers photosynthesis, heats the ocean so water evaporates, and heats the equator more than the poles, which creates wind and ocean currents. Orange stars like Ostra stay stable for tens of billions of years, so life has plenty of time to develop. Veyra also has heat from inside the planet, which powers deep-sea vents, and tidal energy from its two moons, which stirs up the ocean.

Where is the water?

  • Ocean (about 96%): the salty Vael Ocean, about 3.9 km deep on average.
  • Ice (about 2%): the Boreal Ice Cap and the Austral sea ice.
  • Fresh water on land: rivers like the Silvermouth, the Hollow Rift lakes and groundwater underground.
  • In the air: water vapor and clouds.

How does the water cycle work?

Choose the Water cycle layer to see these steps on the map.

  1. Evaporation: Ostra heats the ocean and water turns into vapor. The salt is left behind, so the vapor is fresh water.
  2. Condensation: wind carries the moist air east. When it hits the Crown Range it is pushed up, cools down and forms clouds.
  3. Precipitation: heavy rain falls on the Verdant Slopes, over 3,000 mm per year.
  4. Runoff and groundwater: rainwater flows into the Silvermouth River or soaks into the ground. Plants also release water back into the air (transpiration).
  5. Back to the ocean: the river empties at the delta and the cycle starts again.

On the other side of the mountains, the air has already lost its water, so it sinks and dries out. This rain shadow is why the Ashen Dunes desert is right next to a rainforest.

How do water's properties allow life to exist?

  • It dissolves almost anything. Water carries nutrients through rivers, soil and the inside of cells.
  • It holds a lot of heat. The ocean warms up and cools down slowly, so temperatures on Veyra stay steady between day and night and between seasons.
  • Ice floats. Lakes and polar seas freeze on top, not from the bottom, so animals can survive underneath in winter.
  • It sticks to itself and other things. This lets water climb up through plant stems to reach the leaves.
  • It stays liquid over a wide range. With Veyra's thicker air, water doesn't boil until about 107 °C.

Weathering

Weathering breaks rock into smaller pieces.

  • Mechanical: in the Crown Range and Frostmere Valleys, water freezes in cracks and expands, splitting the rock (frost wedging). Plant roots also grow into cracks. In the desert, hot days and cold nights make rock crack and flake.
  • Chemical: CO₂ mixes with rain to make a weak acid that slowly dissolves rock. Oxygen rusts the iron in volcanic rock, which is why the Kaelor Ashlands have red soil.

How do weathering and erosion support ecosystems?

Rock contains nutrients that living things need, like phosphorus, potassium, calcium and iron. Weathering releases them, and erosion carries them by river to soils, wetlands and the ocean, where they feed plants and plankton. When those organisms die, decomposers return the nutrients to the soil and seafloor. Some sediment eventually gets pulled into the mantle at a trench and comes back up through volcanoes as new rock, so the cycle keeps going.

Weathering also helps control the climate. When Veyra gets warmer, there is more rain and more weathering, which pulls CO₂ out of the air and cools things down. When it gets cooler, weathering slows down, and volcanic CO₂ builds back up and warms it.

Section 3

Life Ingredients

We included all 8 ingredients for life. For each one, here's where it's found on Veyra, why life needs it and how the planet's systems keep it available.

Liquid water hydrosphere

Where
The Vael Ocean, rivers, rift lakes, groundwater and clouds.
Why
Every chemical reaction in a living cell happens in water.
Sustained by
Veyra's distance from Ostra and its greenhouse atmosphere keep it liquid. The water cycle keeps fresh water moving to land. The magnetic field stops it from being stripped into space, and volcanoes add new water vapor.

Energy star + interior

Where
Sunlight from Ostra, heat from deep-sea vents, and tides.
Why
Living things need energy to grow and move.
Sustained by
Plants and plankton use Ostra's light for photosynthesis, and Ostra will shine steadily for billions of years. At the Seam Vent Gardens, bacteria use chemicals from the vents instead (chemosynthesis), which keeps working as long as the plates keep moving.

Carbon all four spheres

Where
CO₂ in the air, dissolved in the ocean, in limestone rock and in every living thing.
Why
Carbon is the building block of sugars, proteins and DNA.
Sustained by
The carbon cycle. Plants take CO₂ from the air, and animals breathe it back out. Shells become limestone, which is subducted at the Ember Trench and released again by Mount Kaelor.

Nitrogen atmosphere

Where
75% of the air, plus soil and seawater.
Why
It is needed to make proteins and DNA.
Sustained by
Lightning and bacteria on plant roots turn nitrogen gas into a form plants can use. Decomposers return it to the soil, other bacteria release it back to the air, and volcanoes add more.

Oxygen atmosphere

Where
22% of the air, dissolved in seawater, and in water and rocks.
Why
Animals use it to get energy from food (respiration), and the ozone layer blocks UV light.
Sustained by
Photosynthesis by forests and ocean plankton keeps refilling it. Waves and tides mix it down into the deep ocean.

Hydrogen hydrosphere

Where
Mostly in water (H₂O) and in all living things.
Why
It is part of water and almost every biological molecule, and it helps hold DNA together.
Sustained by
It stays locked in water. Veyra's stronger gravity and magnetic field keep it from escaping to space. Vents also release hydrogen that deep-sea bacteria use.

Phosphorus geosphere

Where
In rock (especially volcanic and mountain rock), soil and river sediment.
Why
Cells need it for DNA and for ATP, which carries energy in cells.
Sustained by
It only gets released when rock weathers. Veyra's growing mountains and fresh volcanic rock keep supplying it, and the Silvermouth River carries it to the delta and the ocean.

Sulfur geosphere

Where
Volcanic gases, hydrothermal vents and seawater.
Why
It helps proteins hold their shape.
Sustained by
Volcanoes release sulfur that rain washes into soil and the sea. At the vents, bacteria use hydrogen sulfide for energy. Subduction carries sulfur back down so it can be released again.
Integration of Systems

How Veyra's Systems Work Together

On Veyra, the four spheres depend on each other. Each arrow below is one way a sphere affects another.

The main connections

  • Geosphere → Atmosphere: volcanoes like Kaelor release CO₂, water vapor and nitrogen. The Crown Range blocks wind and causes a rain shadow.
  • Atmosphere → Hydrosphere: wind drives evaporation and ocean currents. CO₂ mixes with rain to make weak acid.
  • Hydrosphere → Geosphere: rain, rivers, ice and waves weather and erode rock. Water carried down with subducting plates helps melt rock into magma.
  • Geosphere → Biosphere: weathered rock gives soil its nutrients, volcanic ash makes fertile land, and vents feed deep-sea life.
  • Biosphere → Atmosphere: plants and plankton make oxygen and take in CO₂. Plants also release water vapor.
  • Biosphere → Geosphere: roots break up rock, and shells pile up to form limestone.

Example: following one carbon atom

Choose the Carbon cycle layer to see this loop on the planet.

  1. Mount Kaelor erupts and releases CO₂ into the air. (geosphere → atmosphere)
  2. The CO₂ dissolves in rain and washes into the ocean. (atmosphere → hydrosphere)
  3. Plankton use it to build shells. (hydrosphere → biosphere)
  4. The shells sink and become limestone on the seafloor. (biosphere → geosphere)
  5. The plate carries the limestone into the Ember Trench, where it melts and feeds the volcano again.
What if Veyra's plates stopped moving? Volcanoes would stop adding CO₂, but weathering would keep removing it. Veyra would slowly cool and freeze. The mountains would also wear down flat, and new nutrients would stop being released. Plate tectonics is what keeps all the systems running.
