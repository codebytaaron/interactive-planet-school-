Section 1

Planet Overview & Geology

The planet

Veyra is a little bigger than Earth. One ocean, the Cardinal Ocean, covers most of it. It has two continents, Clark and Sheldon, plus polar ice and island chains. The climate is mild, with rainforests, deserts, wetlands and ice caps.

Active plate tectonics

Veyra's hot interior drives mantle convection, which moves 7 plates about 2–9 cm per year.

Plate interactions

Divergent

Sean Ridge: plates pull apart, and magma makes new seafloor.
Kevin Rift: a continent splitting into a rift valley with lakes.

Convergent

Donovan Trench: an ocean plate sinks under a continent, feeding volcanoes like Mount Suter.
Cos Cob Isles: ocean meets ocean, forming a volcanic island arc.
Manning Range: two continents collide and fold into 8.4 km mountains.

Transform

Ryan Fault: plates slide past each other, causing earthquakes.

Hotspot

Bella Isles: the plate moves over a fixed plume, leaving a chain of islands.

How tectonics supports life

  • Climate: subduction buries carbon, and volcanoes release CO₂. This keeps temperatures balanced.
  • Recycling: crust is destroyed at trenches and remade at ridges, and rising mountains expose fresh, nutrient-rich rock.
  • Habitats: mountains, rift lakes, volcanic soil, islands and vents.
  • Protection: the moving liquid core creates a magnetic field.

Section 2

Atmosphere, Water & Weathering

Atmosphere

75% nitrogen, 22% oxygen, plus argon, water vapor and CO₂. The thick air and greenhouse gases keep Veyra warm. The ozone layer blocks UV light, and the magnetic field stops the air from being stripped away.

Energy source

The star Ostra powers photosynthesis, evaporation and wind. Heat from inside the planet powers deep-sea vents.

Water cycle

  1. Evaporation: Ostra heats the ocean.
  2. Condensation: moist air rises over the Manning Range and forms clouds.
  3. Precipitation: heavy rain falls on the George Forest.
  4. Runoff: water flows down the Matthew River or soaks into the ground.
  5. Return: the river reaches the sea at the delta.

Dry air sinking on the other side of the mountains creates the Massimo Dunes desert (a rain shadow).

Water's properties and life

  • Dissolves nutrients and carries them to living things.
  • Holds heat, so the ocean keeps temperatures steady.
  • Ice floats, so life survives under frozen water.
  • Cohesion lets water move up through plants.

Weathering

  • Mechanical: frost wedging in the William Valleys, and roots cracking rock.
  • Chemical: acid rain dissolves rock, and oxidation turns the Ashlands soil red.

Nutrient cycling

Weathering frees nutrients like phosphorus and iron from rock. Rivers carry them to soil and the ocean, where they feed plants and plankton. Decomposers return them, and subduction and volcanoes recycle the rock.

Integration of Systems

How the Systems Connect

  • Geo → Atmo: volcanoes release gases, and mountains create a rain shadow.
  • Atmo → Hydro: wind drives evaporation, and CO₂ makes rain acidic.
  • Hydro → Geo: water weathers and erodes rock, and helps melt magma.
  • Geo → Bio: weathered rock and ash supply nutrients.
  • Bio → Atmo: plants make O₂ and absorb CO₂.
  • Bio → Geo: roots crack rock, and shells form limestone.

Carbon cycle example

  1. Mount Suter erupts CO₂.
  2. Rain carries the CO₂ to the ocean.
  3. Plankton build shells from it.
  4. The shells become limestone.
  5. Subduction at the trench melts the limestone, which feeds the volcano again.
